Your attention span is forty-seven seconds. And it used to be a lot longer. Psychologist Gloria Mark has spent twenty years tracking how people focus on their screens. In 2004, people stayed on one screen for about two and a half minutes before switching. By 2012, it was seventy-five seconds. Now? Forty-seven seconds. But here's the strange part. Nobody was making them switch. No ping. No message. People interrupted themselves almost as often as they were interrupted by others. Just a sudden urge to check something. Your brain has learned to switch, and now it does it on its own. So next time you feel that itch to check, wait. Just forty-seven seconds more. Follow for more psychology.
