You talked for three days, and you're already imagining a future together. Why does your brain attach so fast? In 1997, psychologist Arthur Aron paired up complete strangers. For forty-five minutes, they asked each other a list of thirty-six questions. They started small. "Would you like to be famous?" And got deeper. "What is your most treasured memory?" By the end, many pairs felt closer to each other than to some of the people in their own lives. One pair later got married. The secret wasn't time. It was opening up, back and forth, a little more each round. That's why a few deep conversations can feel like months. But feeling close isn't the same as knowing someone. So enjoy the spark.
