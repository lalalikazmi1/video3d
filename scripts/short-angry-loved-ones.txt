Why are you patient with strangers, but snap at the people you love most? In the 1980s, psychologist Rena Repetti studied air traffic controllers and their families. On their most stressful days at work, they were calm and controlled with the planes. But at home, they pulled away, or got irritable. Psychologists call this spillover. You hold it together all day, at school, at work, with strangers. And when you get home, the effort stops. The stress comes out on the people who feel safe. Because deep down, you know they'll still love you tomorrow. That's not an excuse. But it is an explanation. So next time you snap, pause. Say "I'm stressed, it's not you." And then say sorry.
