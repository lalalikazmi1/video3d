How did you meet the most important people in your life? Chances are, it wasn't fate. It was distance. Here's the psychology of why we fall for the people who live, work and sit closest to us.

Psychologists call it the proximity effect. In this video we explore the famous MIT Westgate West study, where friendships were shaped by who lived next to the stairs; the Philadelphia marriage records; the police academy where the alphabet decided who became friends; the mere exposure effect; why expecting to see someone makes you like them more; why your worst enemies live close by too; and how proximity works in the age of dating apps and group chats. Then: what it all means for finding friends and love in your own life.
