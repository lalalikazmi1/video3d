That embarrassing thing you did today? Almost nobody noticed. And there's a study to prove it. At Cornell University, psychologists made students wear a T-shirt with a giant picture of Barry Manilow on the front. For a college student, this was social suicide. Then they sent them into a room full of other students. Afterwards, they asked: how many people do you think noticed your shirt? The students guessed half the room. The real number? About a quarter. A giant, embarrassing face on their chest, and most people didn't even see it. Psychologists call this the spotlight effect. You feel like everyone's watching you.
