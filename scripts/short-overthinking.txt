You sent a text three hours ago. No reply. And you've reread it eleven times. Why does your brain do this? In 2016, researchers in London gave people a game where they might get an electric shock. The most stressed people weren't the ones who knew a shock was coming. They were the ones who had a fifty-fifty chance. Not knowing was worse than knowing the bad news. That's what an unanswered message feels like to your brain. A fifty-fifty. So it starts searching for an answer, by replaying everything, over and over. Psychologists call this rumination. It feels like problem solving. But it isn't. It just keeps you stuck in the feeling. So ask yourself one question: can I do anything about this right now? If not, put the phone down.
