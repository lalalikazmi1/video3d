You hear a recording of yourself and think: that's not what I sound like. But it is. When you speak, you hear your voice in two ways. Through the air, like everyone else. And through the bones in your head. Those vibrations make your voice sound deeper and richer, but only to you. A recording strips that away. So you hear a thinner, higher version, and your brain says: that's wrong. But here's the twist. In 2013, psychologists Susan Hughes and Marissa Harrison played people a mix of voice recordings, and secretly included their own. When people didn't realise it was them, they rated their own voice as more attractive than other listeners did. So the voice you hate? Everyone else has been hearing it the whole time.
