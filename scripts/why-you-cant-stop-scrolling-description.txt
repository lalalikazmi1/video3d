It's one in the morning. You said five more minutes. That was an hour ago. You're not weak and you're not lazy: your brain is doing exactly what it was built to do, inside a machine designed to keep you there. Here's the psychology of why you can't stop scrolling.

In this video we explore B. F. Skinner's pigeons and the variable reward schedule, why pull-to-refresh works like a slot machine, Wolfram Schultz's dopamine experiments and the difference between wanting and liking, the UCLA study on teenagers and likes, how infinite scroll removed our stopping cues, the study where people chose electric shocks over sitting alone with their thoughts, the "brain drain" of a phone on your desk, and what happened when students cut social media to 30 minutes a day. Then: five ways to take back control.
