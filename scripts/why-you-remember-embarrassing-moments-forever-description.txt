It's late. You're almost asleep. And then your brain says: "Hey. Remember that thing you said in 2014?" Why does your mind hold on to your most embarrassing moments so tightly, while forgetting where you put your keys five minutes ago? And is anyone else even thinking about them? Here's the psychology of why embarrassment sticks, and why nobody noticed as much as you think.

In this video we explore the famous Barry Manilow T-shirt study and the spotlight effect, the illusion of transparency, why "bad is stronger than good", the Cyberball experiment showing that social rejection lights up the brain like physical pain, how emotion acts as a highlighter for memory, why the cringe replays at 2am, and the surprising research showing that embarrassment can actually make people like you more. Then: five ways to stop paying for the thing you said in 2014.
