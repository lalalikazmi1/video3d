You sit down. Laptop open. Coffee ready. Today you're going to lock in. Twenty minutes later you're watching a video about how pyramids were built, and the task is still on line one. You're not lazy. Here's the psychology of why your brain fights you every time you try to focus, and how to actually lock in.

In this video we explore Gloria Mark's research showing attention on a screen has dropped to about 47 seconds, why we interrupt ourselves, attention residue and the hidden cost of every switch, why one "quick check" can cost you around 23 minutes, why your brain loves novelty, the Harvard study showing our minds wander nearly half the time, why procrastination is really a mood problem, what heavy multitasking does to focus, and what six hours of sleep does to your attention. Then: six ways to lock in.
