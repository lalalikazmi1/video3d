What if you never had to sleep? Eight extra hours, every single day. More time to study, to game, to live. In 1964, a seventeen-year-old named Randy Gardner tried to find out. For a school science project, he stayed awake for eleven days straight. By day two, he struggled to focus his eyes. By day four, he started hallucinating, and believed he was a famous football player. By the end, he struggled to finish simple maths, and to remember what he was doing in the middle of a sentence. Because sleep isn't wasted time. While you sleep, your brain stores the day's memories, and in 2013, scientists found it clears out waste that builds up while you're awake. So those eight hours aren't lost. They're how your brain gets ready for tomorrow. Follow for more psychology.
