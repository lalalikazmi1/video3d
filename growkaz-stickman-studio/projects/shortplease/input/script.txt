Would you write the word "pickle" in a library book, just because a stranger asked you to? In a study by psychologist Vanessa Bohns, about half the people who were asked actually did it. Not because they wanted to. Because saying no felt too awkward. That's the trap. Saying no feels like it will damage the relationship. So you say yes, again and again. But here's the good news. In 2023, researchers Julian Givi and Colleen Kirk studied what happens when you turn down an invitation. People expected the other person to be hurt, angry, and less likely to invite them again. In reality? The other person barely cared. So next time, try it. A kind, simple no. It costs you far less than you think. Follow for more psychology.
