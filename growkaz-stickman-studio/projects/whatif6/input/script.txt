What if you could see your life from the outside? Like watching a movie about yourself. Every argument. Every bad decision. Every time you said yes when you meant no. You'd probably see it all so clearly. And here's the strange part. You already can. In 2014, psychologists Igor Grossmann and Ethan Kross found something called Solomon's paradox. When people thought about a friend's relationship problem, they gave wise, calm advice. But when it was their own problem, that wisdom disappeared. Then they tried one small trick. They asked people to think about their own problem as if it was happening to someone else. And the wisdom came back. So next time you're stuck, step outside. Ask yourself, what would I tell a friend?
