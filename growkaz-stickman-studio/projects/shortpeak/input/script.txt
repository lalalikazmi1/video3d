A great holiday with a terrible last day somehow feels like a bad holiday. Why? In 1993, psychologist Daniel Kahneman asked people to put their hand in painfully cold water. Once for sixty seconds. And once for sixty seconds, plus thirty more, while the water warmed up just a little. Then he asked: which one do you want to repeat? Most people chose the longer one. More pain in total. But it ended better. Kahneman called this the peak-end rule. Your brain doesn't remember the whole experience. It remembers the most intense moment, and how it ended. Everything else gets blurry. So the ending matters more than you think. End your day with something good. End a fight with a hug. End a meeting with a win. Follow for more psychology.
