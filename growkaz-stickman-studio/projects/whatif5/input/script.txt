What if you could delete a memory? That embarrassing moment. That breakup. That one night you wish never happened. Just gone. Scientists have actually come close. Every time you remember something, your brain briefly unlocks the memory, then saves it again. In 2009, researchers in Amsterdam taught people to fear pictures of spiders. Then, while they recalled that fear, they gave them a common heart drug called propranolol. The next day, the fear response was gone. The memory was still there, but the feeling attached to it had faded. But here's the catch. Painful memories are how you learned not to touch the stove, not to trust that person, and how to be careful next time. Delete them, and you lose the lesson too. So maybe the goal isn't to forget. It's to remember, without it hurting. Follow for more psychology.
