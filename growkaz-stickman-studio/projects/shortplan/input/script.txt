You'll do it tomorrow. You've said that for three weeks. Why do you always think you'll have more time? In 1994, psychologist Roger Buehler asked students how long their final thesis would take. On average, they guessed about thirty-four days. It actually took them fifty-five. Then he asked for their worst-case estimate. If everything went wrong. Most of them still missed it. Psychologists Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky called this the planning fallacy. When you plan, you picture the perfect version. No distractions. No problems. No bad days. But real life always has them. So here's the fix. Don't ask, "how long should this take?" Ask, "how long did it take last time?" Your past is a better planner than your hopes.
