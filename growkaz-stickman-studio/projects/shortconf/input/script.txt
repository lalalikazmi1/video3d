The loudest person in the room isn't always the smartest. So why do we believe them? In 2012, psychologist Cameron Anderson and his colleagues put students into groups to solve problems together. Some of them were overconfident. They thought they were much better than they actually were. You'd expect the group to notice. They didn't. The overconfident people were rated as more competent, more respected, and more likely to be seen as leaders. Even though they weren't any better. Why? Because confidence looks like competence. For our brains, sounding sure is a shortcut for being right. And most of the time, we don't check. So next time someone sounds certain, ask one question: what's their track record?
