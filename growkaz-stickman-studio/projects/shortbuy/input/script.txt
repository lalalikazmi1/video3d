You didn't need it. You didn't even want it. Until you saw the other option. Behavioural economist Dan Ariely tested a real magazine offer on his students. Online only: fifty-nine dollars. Print only: one hundred and twenty-five. Print and online: also one hundred and twenty-five. Nobody chose print only. Eighty-four percent chose print and online. Then he removed the print-only option. Suddenly, most people chose the cheap online version instead. That useless middle option was a decoy. It wasn't there to be bought. It was there to make the expensive one look like a bargain. And it's everywhere. Small, medium, and large popcorn. Three phone plans. "Was ninety-nine, now forty-nine." So next time, cover up the other options, and ask: would I still want this on its own? Follow for more psychology.
