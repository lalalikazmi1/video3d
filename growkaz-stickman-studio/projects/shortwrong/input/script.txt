Why do some people never admit they're wrong, even when the proof is right in front of them? In 1954, psychologist Leon Festinger secretly joined a group who believed the world would end in a great flood on December twenty-first. Some of them quit their jobs. Gave away everything. Then the day came. And nothing happened. You'd expect them to give up. Instead, many believed even harder. They decided their faith had saved the world, and started trying to recruit more people. Festinger called this cognitive dissonance. When the truth clashes with who you think you are, it feels painful. And changing your story is easier than admitting you were wrong.
